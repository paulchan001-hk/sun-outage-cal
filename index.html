Sun Outage Calculator

Estimate the dates and local times when the Sun passes through a receive antenna beam pointed at a geostationary satellite. Any orbital slot. C, Ku and Ka band.

The actual outage depends on signal level, modulation, antenna size, pointing accuracy and the model used. This tool is a geometric estimate. The results may differ from the observed event by about 5 to 10 minutes. Satellite inclination and station-keeping are not modelled.

Method and assumptions

The satellite is treated as geostationary at zero inclination. Look angles use Earth radius 6,378.137 km and GEO radius 42,164.17 km. Azimuth is from true north, clockwise.

Half-power beamwidth is θ = k λ / D, with λ = 0.299792458 / f(GHz) metres and k = 70 unless changed.

Outage angle is AOA = 11/(D×f) + 0.25°, where 0.25° is the solar radius. Event days and GMT clock times use their equinox geometry, equation of time, and solar-noon base. Known fleet slots use the script’s exact GMT constants. Any other slot uses 12:00 GMT minus longitude/15. Frequency and orbital slot remain user inputs, including Ka.

Not included: inclination, eccentricity, rain fade, link margin, polarisation, or the difference between a 3 dB geometric crossing and the C/N at which a particular carrier fails.

Batch CSV

Each row is one station. Columns: id, site, lat, lat_hemisphere, lon, lon_hemisphere, orbital_slot_east, year, band, frequency_ghz, antenna_m, timezone. Use North or South, and East or West, as whole words. Band is C, Ku, Ka or Custom.
